## 1. Purpose

This document defines the approved population, accessible population, sampling frame, sampling approach, sample-size strategy, stratification considerations, supplementary collection procedures and audit requirements for SkillSignal SA.

The sampling strategy establishes how qualifying technology job advertisements will be identified, assessed, deduplicated and included in the analytical dataset.

It also defines the safeguards required to prevent misleading representation of the South African technology labour market.

The strategy is designed to support reproducible collection within the project's:

- Zero-budget constraint.
- Individual ownership model.
- Ten-week baseline development schedule.
- Fixed retrospective twelve-month research period.
- Approved source permissions and acquisition restrictions.
- Aggregate publication requirements.

---

## 2. Sampling Principles

The sampling strategy follows these principles:

1. **Evidence over assumptions:** Sampling decisions must be supported by documented evidence.
2. **Defensible coverage:** The project should collect qualifying advertisements accessible through approved sources.
3. **Transparency:** Source coverage, collection procedures and limitations must be disclosed.
4. **Reproducibility:** Collection and selection procedures must be documented sufficiently for review.
5. **Canonical records:** Each unique advertised opportunity must have one canonical analytical record.
6. **Natural representation:** The observed distribution of qualifying advertisements must not be artificially balanced.
7. **Targeted supplementation:** Underrepresented areas may receive additional searches where justified.
8. **Data quality:** Records must satisfy eligibility and provenance requirements.
9. **No fabricated observations:** Missing categories or sample-size shortfalls must be reported honestly.
10. **Analysis-specific adequacy:** Each research question must be assessed according to its own data requirements.

## 3. Target Population

### 3.1 Definition

The target population consists of:

**All qualifying technology employment opportunities advertised during the approved rolling twelve-month research period that are accessible to candidates working in South Africa.**

The target population covers:

- All nine South African provinces.
- Major cities and smaller towns.
- South African-based remote positions.
- Hybrid and on-site positions.
- Qualifying international remote opportunities explicitly open to South African candidates.
- All seven approved technology job families.
- All approved qualifying employment types.

### 3.2 Approved Job Families

| Code | Job Family |
|---|---|
| JF01 | Software Development |
| JF02 | Data and Analytics |
| JF03 | Cybersecurity |
| JF04 | IT Infrastructure and Support |
| JF05 | Cloud and DevOps |
| JF06 | Quality Assurance and Testing |
| JF07 | Business Technology |

Advertisements may receive multiple job-family assignments where their responsibilities and requirements genuinely overlap.

### 3.3 Employment Types

The target population includes qualifying:

- Permanent positions.
- Fixed-term positions.
- Temporary positions.
- Graduate programmes.
- Graduate positions.
- Internships.
- Learnerships.
- Apprenticeships.
- Part-time positions.
- Genuine freelance opportunities.
- Project-based contracts.

Freelance and project-based opportunities must represent genuine paid employment or contracted services.

Volunteer positions, unpaid speculative projects and training courses without employment opportunities are excluded.

## 4. Accessible Population

### 4.1 Definition

The accessible population is the subset of the target population that can be discovered and assessed through approved sources and permitted acquisition methods.

It is constrained by:

- Public accessibility.
- Source availability.
- Source permissions.
- Historical advertisement availability.
- Search functionality.
- Acquisition restrictions.
- Data completeness.
- Project budget.
- Available collection time.
- Technical feasibility.

### 4.2 Candidate Source Categories

Potential source categories include:

- Public recruitment platforms.
- Employer career pages.
- Publicly accessible job listings.
- Other authorised recruitment and employment information sources.

These are candidate source categories, not approved individual sources.

Final source selection, source-specific permissions and acquisition methods remain subject to Section C.

### 4.3 Sampling Limitation

The accessible population must not be described as equivalent to the complete South African technology labour market.

The project can only make evidence-based claims about advertisements identified through its approved and documented sources.

## 5. Sampling Frame

### 5.1 Definition

The sampling frame is the consolidated collection of discovered job advertisements that contain sufficient information to assess:

- Advertisement identity.
- Source provenance.
- Publication period.
- Geographic eligibility.
- Technology job-family eligibility.
- Potential duplication.
- Research inclusion criteria.

### 5.2 Sampling Frame Construction

The sampling frame will be constructed through the following process:

1. Discover candidate advertisements through approved sources.
2. Record source-level provenance.
3. Create candidate acquisition records.
4. Validate the source and publication information.
5. Assess geographic eligibility.
6. Assess employment and technology job-family eligibility.
7. Apply research-period rules.
8. Identify uncertain or conflicting records.
9. Review potential duplicates.
10. Establish one canonical record per unique opportunity.
11. Retain documented exclusions and their reasons.
12. Finalise the eligible analytical sampling frame.

### 5.3 Candidate Record States

Candidate records may be classified as:

- Candidate — awaiting assessment.
- Eligible — awaiting duplicate review.
- Eligible — canonical record.
- Confirmed duplicate.
- Ineligible — documented exclusion reason.
- Under review — unresolved eligibility.
- Under review — unresolved date.
- Under review — uncertain duplicate.
- Invalid or unverifiable.

Records under review must not silently enter the final analytical dataset.

## 6. Unit of Analysis

### 6.1 Approved Unit

The unit of analysis is:

**One unique job advertisement representing one advertised employment opportunity.**

Each canonical opportunity receives a unique internal advertisement identifier.

The identifier must remain stable and must not depend on:

- Job title.
- Employer name.
- Source platform.
- Source URL.
- Publication date.

### 6.2 Canonical Advertisement Approach

The project adopts a single canonical advertisement per unique opportunity.

Where the same opportunity appears on multiple sources or is reposted:

- Maintain one canonical analytical record.
- Preserve source provenance where permitted.
- Retain duplicate evidence.
- Link additional publication instances to the canonical record.
- Record repost dates.
- Preserve material changes where relevant.
- Retain uncertainty for manual review.

### 6.3 Distinct Opportunities

Genuinely different advertised vacancies must remain separate records even where they share:

- An employer.
- A job title.
- A job description.
- A recruitment platform.
- Similar publication dates.

Uncertain cases must be reviewed rather than automatically merged.

### 6.4 Advertisement-Level and Relationship-Level Data

One canonical advertisement may be associated with multiple:

- Job families.
- Skills.
- Qualifications.
- Certifications.
- Experience requirements.
- Locations.
- Employment characteristics.

These relationships must not create additional unique advertisement counts.

## 7. Sampling Approach

### 7.1 Approved Strategy

The project adopts a hybrid census and stratified analytical approach.

The collection strategy is:

**Collect all eligible advertisements discoverable through approved sources, construct the canonical analytical dataset, analyse its natural distribution and use stratified subsets or controlled comparisons where appropriate.**

### 7.2 Accessible Census Component

The project will attempt to collect all qualifying advertisements discoverable through the approved sources within the approved research period.

This does not constitute a census of every South African technology vacancy.

The appropriate description is:

**A non-probability census of accessible eligible job advertisements.**

### 7.3 Stratified Analytical Component

Stratified comparisons may be used to investigate differences across:

- Job families.
- Provinces and geographic categories.
- Employer geography.
- Employment types.
- Experience and seniority.
- Work arrangements.
- Source platforms.
- Publication months.

These variables are potential grouping or comparison dimensions.

Their inclusion does not automatically establish formal probability-sampling strata.

### 7.4 No Artificial Balancing

The project will not artificially balance the dataset to produce equal numbers across:

- Job families.
- Provinces.
- Employment types.
- Experience categories.
- Work arrangements.
- Sources.
- Publication months.

The natural distribution of the accessible advertisement sample must be preserved.

## 8. Sample Size Strategy

### 8.1 Approved Approach

The project adopts a feasibility-based sample-size strategy.

No fixed numerical sample target is approved before the source pilot.

The target will be determined after initial source assessment and pilot acquisition.

### 8.2 Factors Determining the Target

The sample target must consider:

1. Number of approved sources.
2. Unique eligible advertisement volume.
3. Source coverage.
4. Historical data availability.
5. Completeness of critical variables.
6. Data quality.
7. Duplicate rates.
8. Geographic coverage.
9. Job-family coverage.
10. Employment-type coverage.
11. Available project time.
12. Zero-budget constraints.

### 8.3 Target Justification

The final target must be documented before full-scale acquisition.

The justification must explain:

- What sources were assessed.
- What collection volume was feasible.
- What pilot findings were observed.
- What limitations affected collection.
- Why the selected target is reasonable.

### 8.4 No Global Minimum Sample Requirement

A single minimum sample size will not be applied to every research question.

Adequacy must be assessed separately for each analysis.

The project must not collect unsuitable or unverifiable advertisements merely to reach a numerical target.

---

## 9. Targeted Supplementary Collection

### 9.1 Approved Approach

The project adopts natural representation combined with targeted supplementary collection.

Additional searches may be conducted where the initial accessible sample underrepresents relevant:

- Job families.
- Geographic areas.
- Employment types.
- Experience categories.
- Graduate opportunities.
- Other important analytical categories.

### 9.2 Supplementary Collection Rules

Supplementary collection must:

1. Use approved sources.
2. Follow approved acquisition methods.
3. Apply the same eligibility requirements.
4. Follow the same research-period rules.
5. Follow the same duplicate-handling process.
6. Preserve complete provenance.
7. Be clearly identifiable.
8. Avoid artificial quotas.
9. Avoid introducing unsuitable advertisements.
10. Document unsuccessful searches and coverage limitations where relevant.

### 9.3 Collection Purpose Indicator

All canonical analytical advertisements must include a collection-purpose indicator.

Approved values:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `primary_collection` | Advertisement acquired through the initial planned collection. |
| `targeted_supplementary` | Advertisement acquired through a targeted supplementary search. |

### 9.4 Dataset Integration

All qualifying supplementary advertisements will be merged into the same canonical analytical dataset.

The collection-purpose indicator must be retained for analysis and reporting.

Supplementary advertisements must not be excluded merely because they were collected through targeted searches.

However, supplementary collection must be disclosed so that its effect on the observed sample distribution can be assessed.

| SV-05 | Experience and seniority | Experience categories and seniority levels |
| SV-06 | Work arrangement | On-site, hybrid, fully remote, flexible, not disclosed |
| SV-07 | Source platform | Approved source identifier |
| SV-08 | Publication month | November 2025 through October 2026 |

### 10.1 Grouping Safeguards

- Missing values must remain distinguishable from confirmed categories.
- Multiple values must be preserved where applicable.
- Ambiguous values must be flagged for review.
- Employer geography must remain distinct from work location.
- Grouping variables must not be treated as formal sampling strata unless a separate methodological decision justifies that treatment.
- Comparisons must account for uneven group sizes.

## 11. Sampling Methodology

### 11.1 Approved Sampling Type

The project uses transparent non-probability sampling.

The final dataset must be described as:

**A feasibility-based non-probability census of accessible eligible technology job advertisements, supplemented by targeted collection where justified.**

### 11.2 Representativeness

The project must not claim that its dataset is statistically representative of all South African technology vacancies.

The research must:

- Identify the sources used.
- Document source coverage.
- Report collection dates and procedures.
- Describe the accessible population.
- Monitor category representation.
- Report missingness.
- Disclose source and selection limitations.
- Avoid unsupported generalisations.

### 11.3 Weighting

Weighting adjustments are not part of the approved sampling strategy.

The project will not apply population weights or artificially adjust observations to produce an assumed market distribution.

Any future proposal for weighting would require a separate methodological justification and formal approval.

## 12. Analysis-Specific Sample Adequacy

### 12.1 Approved Principle

The adequacy of the dataset must be assessed separately for each research question and statistical analysis.

A dataset may be suitable for one analysis but inadequate for another.

For example:

- Overall skill frequency may be reliable even if one job family has few observations.
- Salary disclosure may be analysable even if usable numerical salary values are insufficient.
- Provincial summaries may be possible while detailed province-by-family comparisons remain unsuitable.

### 12.2 Adequacy Assessment Criteria

Each analysis should assess:

- Number of eligible unique advertisements.
- Number of observations per group.
- Relevant variable completeness.
- Missingness.
- Classification and extraction confidence.
- Category distribution.
- Expected statistical frequencies.
- Independence and overlapping assignments.
- Statistical assumptions.
- Practical interpretability.

### 12.3 Alternative Analytical Approaches

When data is insufficient:

- Use descriptive analysis where appropriate.
- Consider suitable exact statistical tests.
- Simplify models only where methodologically justified.
- Combine categories only when conceptually compatible.
- Suppress unsupported comparisons.
- Report limitations.
- Record analyses that cannot be performed.

Category consolidation must never be undertaken solely to obtain statistical significance.

### 12.4 Analysis Statuses

Use the following approved statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Completed | Analysis successfully performed. |
| Completed with limitations | Analysis performed, but limitations affect interpretation. |
| Descriptive only | Inferential analysis was not justified. |
| Modified | Original method replaced by a suitable alternative. |
| Not performed — insufficient data | Available data cannot support the analysis. |
| Not performed — assumption failure | Required assumptions could not be adequately addressed. |
| Pending review | Data or classification requires further validation. |

Insufficient data must not be interpreted as evidence of no relationship or no difference.

## 13. Audit Trail and Sampling Documentation

### 13.1 Approved Audit Requirements

The project must maintain an auditable record of:

1. Source discovery.
2. Source assessment.
3. Source approval and permission status.
4. Acquisition method.
5. Collection date.
6. Search criteria.
7. Collection batch.
8. Candidate advertisements.
9. Source provenance.
10. Eligibility decisions.
11. Publication-date decisions.
12. Duplicate decisions.
13. Canonical advertisement creation.
14. Job-family classification.
15. Supplementary searches.
16. Sampling-frame changes.
17. Exclusions and reasons.
18. Manual corrections.
19. Final coverage assessment.

### 13.2 Batch Logging

Collection must be traceable through batch records.

A batch record should identify:

- Source.
- Acquisition method.
- Collection date.
- Search criteria.
- Collection batch identifier.
- Collection purpose.
- Number of candidate records.
- Number of eligible records.
- Number of exclusions.
- Number of confirmed duplicates.
- Outstanding review cases.

Exact physical schemas and field implementations will be finalised during the technical architecture and database design phases.

### 13.3 Exclusion Codes

Exclusion reasons must be consistently documented.

Potential exclusion categories include:

- Outside research period.
- Outside approved job families.
- Geographic ineligibility.
- No specific employment opportunity.
- Training without employment.
- Volunteer or unpaid speculative opportunity.
- Invalid or unverifiable record.
- Confirmed duplicate.
- Prohibited acquisition method.
- Other documented reason.

Uncertain records must be held for review rather than silently excluded.

### 13.4 No Silent Deletions

Records must not be silently deleted from the sampling process.

Where records are removed from the analytical dataset:

- Preserve permitted evidence.
- Record the exclusion reason.
- Maintain traceability to the original candidate record.
- Record the decision date.
- Record manual changes.

Retention of source evidence remains subject to approved governance and source restrictions.

## 15. Sampling Limitations and Bias

### 15.1 Non-Probability Sampling Bias

The accessible sample may differ from the full population of South African technology job advertisements.

### 15.2 Platform Coverage Bias

Some employers or occupations may be more visible on certain recruitment platforms.

### 15.3 Historical Availability Bias

Older advertisements may be less accessible than recent advertisements.

### 15.4 Geographic Coverage Bias

Certain provinces or locations may be more visible in the approved sources.

### 15.5 Job-Family Coverage Bias

Some technology job families may have more accessible advertisements than others.

### 15.6 Employment-Type Coverage Bias

Permanent and full-time opportunities may be more visible than freelance, internship or project-based opportunities.

### 15.7 Duplicate Detection Limitations

Different publication instances may be difficult to identify as the same underlying opportunity.

### 15.8 Missing Information

Advertisements with limited disclosure may restrict some analyses.

### 15.9 Supplementary Collection Effects

Targeted supplementary searches may change the natural distribution of the final accessible sample.

Their effect must be identifiable through the collection-purpose indicator.

### 15.10 Interpretation

These limitations must be discussed when reporting findings.

The project must not make unsupported population-level claims or suggest that the dataset captures every advertised technology opportunity in South Africa.
