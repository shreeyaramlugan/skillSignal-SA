## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the standards for selecting,
applying and reporting statistical methods in SkillSignal SA.

Methods must be appropriate to the research questions,
data characteristics and limitations.

## 2. Analytical Principles

- Use methods appropriate to the measurement level.
- Inspect data quality before analysis.
- Document assumptions and limitations.
- Distinguish descriptive from inferential results.
- Avoid unsupported causal claims.
- Report denominators and missing data where relevant.
- Preserve reproducibility.
- Do not fabricate or selectively misrepresent findings.

## 4. Statistical Assumptions

Where applicable, analysis must consider:
- Independence of observations.
- Distributional assumptions.
- Sample size and statistical power.
- Missingness mechanisms.
- Unequal group sizes.
- Multiple comparisons.
- Confounding and selection bias.
- Measurement and classification error.

The appropriate treatment depends on the selected method.

## 5. Reporting Standards

Where applicable, results should include:
- Sample size.
- Relevant denominators.
- Descriptive summaries.
- Effect sizes.
- Confidence intervals.
- Test statistics.
- P-values.
- Assumption checks.
- Limitations and interpretation.

Statistical significance must not be presented as equivalent
to practical importance.

## 6. Reproducibility

Analytical work should:
- Use version-controlled scripts or notebooks.
- Record software and package versions.
- Document transformations.
- Record random seeds where relevant.
- Separate exploratory work from final analysis.
- Retain sufficient information to reproduce published results.

## 7. Predictive Modelling

Predictive modelling is an unresolved proposal.

It must not be added merely to increase technical complexity.
Any proposed model must have:
- A defensible prediction objective.
- Suitable features and outcome variables.
- Adequate data.
- A justified validation strategy.
- Appropriate evaluation metrics.
- Clear limitations.

The decision is reserved for Sections F and G.
