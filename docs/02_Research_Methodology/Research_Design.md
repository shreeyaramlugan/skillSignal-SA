## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the approved research design for SkillSignal SA.

It establishes the research problem, justification, aim, objectives, scope, geographic boundaries, research period and data collection framework.

It provides the methodological foundation for subsequent data sourcing, acquisition, processing, classification, statistical analysis and publication.

The document translates the approved project charter into a structured research design that can guide implementation.

### 1.1 Research Design Objectives

The research design aims to:

1. Establish the problem that SkillSignal SA intends to investigate.
2. Define the research aim and measurable objectives.
3. Establish the research questions and hypotheses that guide the investigation.
4. Define the population, geographic scope and employment opportunities included.
5. Establish the research period and publication-date eligibility rules.
6. Define the unit of analysis and boundaries of the investigation.
7. Establish the methodological foundation for sampling and data acquisition.
8. Ensure that subsequent analytical decisions remain consistent with the approved research scope.
9. Establish limitations and safeguards against unsupported conclusions.

## 2. Research Background and Context

Technology occupations are represented across multiple recruitment platforms, employer career pages and other publicly accessible recruitment channels.

Information contained in technology job advertisements is often fragmented and inconsistently structured. Advertisements can differ substantially in how they describe:

- Technical and professional skills.
- Educational qualifications.
- Professional certifications.
- Experience and seniority.
- Graduate and entry-level accessibility.
- Salary and remuneration.
- Geographic requirements.
- Remote, hybrid and on-site working arrangements.
- Employment types and working conditions.

This inconsistency makes it difficult to compare advertised requirements across technology occupations and to identify meaningful patterns in the available information.

South African students, graduates, job seekers, recruiters, employers and educational institutions may benefit from structured information about the requirements and characteristics communicated in technology job advertisements.

SkillSignal SA addresses this research opportunity by applying data acquisition, data engineering, classification and statistical analysis techniques to accessible South African technology job advertisements.

The project will transform collected advertisement information into structured analytical data and aggregate labour-market intelligence.

The research is deliberately based on advertised opportunities rather than verified recruitment outcomes.

## 3. Research Problem and Justification

### 3.1 Research Problem

There is a need for a structured, evidence-based understanding of the South African technology labour market, particularly the skills, qualifications, experience requirements, employment opportunities, salary transparency and geographic patterns reflected in technology job advertisements.

Existing information is fragmented across recruitment platforms and inconsistently presented, limiting the ability of stakeholders to identify and compare meaningful labour-market patterns.

SkillSignal SA seeks to address this information gap by developing a reproducible data analytics solution that transforms job advertisement data into accessible technology labour-market intelligence.

### 3.2 Problem Context

The research addresses several information challenges:

1. **Fragmented information:** Technology job advertisements are distributed across different recruitment channels.
2. **Inconsistent terminology:** Similar skills, occupations and qualifications may be described using different terms.
3. **Variable levels of disclosure:** Advertisements differ in the amount of information provided about salary, qualifications and experience.
4. **Classification difficulties:** Job titles do not always accurately represent the technical responsibilities of an advertised role.
5. **Geographic variation:** Opportunities may be advertised for specific cities, provinces, multiple locations or remote working arrangements.
6. **Graduate accessibility:** Advertisements may use inconsistent terminology to communicate graduate, junior and entry-level requirements.
7. **Limited comparability:** Differences in structure and completeness make systematic comparisons difficult.
8. **Temporal variation:** Historical advertisements may be difficult to recover and compare consistently.

### 3.3 Research Justification

The project is justified by its potential to:

- Improve the accessibility of structured information about advertised technology requirements.
- Identify frequently requested technical and professional skills.
- Compare advertised requirements across technology job families.
- Investigate qualifications, certifications and experience requirements.
- Examine graduate and entry-level accessibility.
- Explore the geographic distribution of advertised opportunities.
- Investigate salary disclosure and comparable advertised remuneration.
- Examine employment arrangements and working conditions.
- Demonstrate reproducible data analytics and research practices.
- Produce a technically advanced portfolio project supported by documented evidence.

### 3.4 Research Boundary

The research investigates patterns in qualifying job advertisements.

It does not directly measure:

- Actual employee recruitment or hiring outcomes.
- The number of positions successfully filled.
- The complete population of South African technology vacancies.
- Actual employee salaries or compensation received.
- The real-world performance or competence of applicants.
- The complete supply of technology skills among job seekers.
- Employer recruitment decisions that are not disclosed in advertisements.

Job advertisements are treated as evidence of advertised requirements and opportunities, not direct evidence of actual employment outcomes.

## 4. Research Aim

To develop and apply a reproducible data analytics framework for investigating the South African technology labour market through the systematic collection, processing and analysis of technology job advertisements, generating evidence-based insights into skills demand, qualifications, experience requirements, employment opportunities, salary transparency, geographic distribution and temporal trends.

### 5.1 Research Orientation

The project adopts an applied, quantitative and analytical research orientation.

It will use structured information extracted from job advertisements to produce descriptive, comparative and inferential insights where the available data supports them.

The research may incorporate automated classification and extraction techniques, but automated outputs must be validated and their limitations documented.

## 6. Research Questions and Hypotheses

The research is guided by twelve approved research questions and seven approved hypotheses.

Their complete formulations, including null and alternative hypotheses, are maintained in:

`docs/02_Research_Methodology/Research_Questions_and_Hypotheses.md`

The approved research questions cover:

1. Overall technology skills demand.
2. Differences in skill demand across job families.
3. Skills combinations and relationships.
4. Qualification and certification requirements.
5. Experience and seniority requirements.
6. Graduate and entry-level opportunities.
7. Geographic opportunity distribution.
8. Salary disclosure.
9. Advertised salary patterns.
10. Employment arrangements and working conditions.
11. Temporal patterns in advertised requirements.
12. Multivariable relationships between job characteristics and advertised requirements.

The seven hypotheses investigate skill distributions, qualifications, experience, geographic distribution, salary disclosure, advertised salary distributions and relationships between job characteristics and skills demand.

Hypothesis testing will only be performed where the dataset and statistical assumptions provide sufficient support.

## 7. Geographic Scope

### 7.1 Primary Geographic Coverage

The research covers all nine provinces of South Africa:

1. Eastern Cape
2. Free State
3. Gauteng
4. KwaZulu-Natal
5. Limpopo
6. Mpumalanga
7. Northern Cape
8. North West
9. Western Cape

The investigation includes opportunities advertised for:

- Major South African cities.
- Smaller towns and regional locations.
- Province-specific locations.
- Multiple South African locations.
- South African-based remote positions.
- Hybrid working arrangements.
- On-site working arrangements.

An advertisement does not need to be located in a major metropolitan area to qualify.

### 7.2 International Remote Opportunities

International employers may be included where an advertisement explicitly permits candidates to work from South Africa.

An international remote opportunity qualifies only when the advertised eligibility conditions establish that candidates located in South Africa may apply and perform the work remotely from South Africa.

Examples:

| Opportunity | Treatment |
|---|---|
| South African employer advertising a local position | Include |
| South African employer advertising a remote position | Include |
| International employer explicitly accepting South African remote applicants | Include separately |
| International position restricted to US residents | Exclude |
| International position restricted to EU residents | Exclude |
| International position with unclear geographic eligibility | Hold for review |

Qualifying international remote opportunities must be distinguishable from domestic opportunities in the analytical dataset and reporting.

### 7.3 Geographic Variables

The research should capture, where available:

- Employer country.
- Work country.
- Province.
- City or locality.
- Geographic category.
- Work arrangement.
- Remote eligibility.
- International remote status.
- South African work eligibility.
- Location restrictions.
- Relocation requirements.

Employer location must not be assumed to be the work location.

A remote opportunity must not automatically be assigned to a province.

## 8. Technology Job Families

The research uses seven approved technology job families.

| Code | Job Family | General Scope |
|---|---|---|
| JF01 | Software Development | Software engineering, application development, web development and related programming occupations. |
| JF02 | Data and Analytics | Data analysis, business intelligence, data engineering, data science and related analytical occupations. |
| JF03 | Cybersecurity | Security analysis, security engineering, information security, penetration testing and related security occupations. |
| JF04 | IT Infrastructure and Support | IT support, systems administration, networking, infrastructure operations and related technical support occupations. |
| JF05 | Cloud and DevOps | Cloud engineering, platform engineering, DevOps, site reliability and related cloud infrastructure occupations. |
| JF06 | Quality Assurance and Testing | Software testing, quality assurance, test automation and related software quality occupations. |
| JF07 | Business Technology | Business systems, enterprise applications, technology consulting, business analysis and related technology-enabled business roles. |

### 8.1 Multiple Job-Family Classification

An advertisement may be assigned to multiple job families when its responsibilities and requirements genuinely overlap.

Classification must be based on evidence from the advertisement, particularly:

- Responsibilities.
- Required technical skills.
- Technical environment.
- Role requirements.
- Occupational purpose.

Job titles alone are insufficient to determine classification.

### 8.2 Analytical Counting Safeguard

The project must distinguish between:

- Unique advertisements.
- Job-family assignments.

One advertisement assigned to two job families remains one unique advertisement but contributes to both family-specific advertisement counts.

Consequently, family assignment totals may exceed the total number of unique advertisements.

Family assignment counts must never be presented as unique vacancies without explicitly explaining the overlap.

Detailed classification rules, confidence requirements and review procedures will be documented in the relevant classification specifications.

## 9. Employment and Opportunity Scope

### 9.1 Included Employment Types

The research includes qualifying:

- Permanent employment.
- Fixed-term employment.
- Temporary employment.
- Graduate programmes.
- Graduate positions.
- Internships.
- Learnerships.
- Apprenticeships.
- Part-time opportunities.
- Genuine freelance opportunities.
- Project-based contract opportunities.

Freelance and project-based opportunities must represent genuine paid employment or contracted services.

### 9.2 Excluded Opportunities

The research excludes:

- Volunteer positions.
- Unpaid speculative projects.
- Training courses without an employment opportunity.
- Generic company promotions.
- Recruitment advertisements that do not identify a specific vacancy.
- Opportunities outside the approved technology job families.
- International positions explicitly excluding South African candidates.
- Advertisements outside the approved research period.
- Invalid or unverifiable records.
- Confirmed duplicate copies after canonicalisation.
- Records acquired through prohibited methods.

### 9.3 Missing Information

An advertisement must not be excluded solely because it does not disclose:

- Salary.
- Skills.
- Qualifications.
- Certifications.
- Experience requirements.
- Benefits.
- Working conditions.

Missing information is analytically relevant and must be represented accurately.

The absence of a qualification statement, for example, does not establish that no qualification is required.

## 10. Unit of Analysis

The unit of analysis is:

**One unique technology job advertisement representing one advertised employment opportunity.**

Each unique opportunity must have one stable internal advertisement identifier.

The canonical record may be associated with multiple:

- Sources.
- Job-family assignments.
- Skills.
- Qualifications.
- Certifications.
- Experience requirements.
- Locations.
- Employment characteristics.

### 10.1 Duplicate Handling

The project will maintain one canonical analytical record for each unique opportunity.

Where the same opportunity appears across multiple platforms or as a repost:

- Retain source provenance where permitted.
- Record duplicate evidence.
- Link duplicate publication instances to the canonical record.
- Preserve meaningful repost information.
- Record material changes.
- Keep genuinely different vacancies separate.
- Send uncertain duplicate cases for manual review.

The detailed sampling and duplicate-handling framework is maintained in:

`docs/02_Research_Methodology/Sampling_Strategy.md`

## 11. Research Period

### 11.1 Approved Research Window

The research uses a fixed retrospective twelve-month period.

| Attribute | Approved Value |
|---|---|
| Research start date | 1 November 2025 |
| Research end date | 31 October 2026 |
| Duration | 12 months |
| Inclusion rule | Both start and end dates inclusive |
| Cutoff | 31 October 2026 inclusive |

Advertisements published between 1 November 2025 and 31 October 2026 qualify for the research period, subject to all other inclusion requirements.

Advertisements published during November 2026 fall outside the research window.

### 11.2 Publication-Date Eligibility

The original publication date determines research-period eligibility.

An advertisement discovered or collected after 31 October 2026 may still qualify if its original publication date can be verified as falling within the approved research window.

The date of collection must never be substituted for the original publication date.

Advertisements with missing or ambiguous publication dates must be held for review rather than automatically included or excluded.

### 11.3 Historical Advertisement Recovery

The project adopts a hybrid historical recovery approach.

Potential historical advertisements may be recovered through permitted:

- Recruitment platform archives.
- Employer career-page records.
- Authorised public records.
- Other approved sources.

Historical records must satisfy the same eligibility, provenance and date-verification requirements as other advertisements.

The project must not reconstruct missing advertisements or fabricate historical publication dates.

Where historical records cannot be recovered, the limitation must be documented.

## 12. Date and Temporal Variables

The research recognises six distinct date fields.

| Variable | Definition |
|---|---|
| `publication_date` | Original date on which the advertisement was published. |
| `closing_date` | Advertised application deadline. |
| `first_seen_date` | First date the project discovered the advertisement. |
| `collection_date` | Date the advertisement was acquired into the project dataset. |
| `last_verified_date` | Most recent date on which the advertisement or its source details were checked. |
| `repost_date` | Confirmed date on which the opportunity was reposted. |

### 12.1 Date Validation Rules

1. Preserve original date wording where available.
2. Standardise verified dates consistently.
3. Retain evidence supporting date interpretation.
4. Record date confidence and review status.
5. Do not substitute closing dates or collection dates for publication dates.
6. Hold ambiguous publication dates for review.
7. Exclude confirmed out-of-period advertisements.
8. Distinguish a repost from a genuinely new vacancy.
9. Record historical recovery limitations.
10. Maintain the same research-period rules across all approved sources.

### 12.2 Derived Temporal Variables

Potential derived variables include:

- Publication month.
- Publication quarter.
- Publication year.
- Advertisement age.
- Days until closing.

Derived date calculations must use a documented reference date where applicable.

Temporal analysis must be based on publication dates, not discovery or collection activity.

## 13. Data Collection Framework

### 13.1 Collection Approach

The project uses a hybrid data acquisition strategy.

The approved research period is fixed and retrospective, while collection will be conducted as one planned acquisition exercise across approved sources.

Multiple sources, queries and acquisition batches may be used within that exercise.

Collection must remain subject to:

- Source accessibility.
- Permitted use.
- Documented acquisition methods.
- Zero-budget constraints.
- Data provenance requirements.
- The approved geographic and occupational scope.

### 13.2 Collection Procedure

The intended collection workflow is:

1. Identify candidate data sources.
2. Evaluate source relevance and coverage.
3. Assess accessibility, permissions and acquisition restrictions.
4. Document approved sources and methods.
5. Identify candidate job advertisements.
6. Record acquisition provenance.
7. Validate publication dates.
8. Assess research eligibility.
9. Review duplicates and reposts.
10. Establish canonical advertisement records.
11. Classify qualifying records.
12. Document uncertain and excluded records.
13. Conduct final coverage and quality assessments.

Final source selection and acquisition implementation are addressed in Section C.

### 13.3 Collection Completion

The acquisition exercise may be considered complete when:

- Planned approved sources have been assessed and their feasible acquisition completed.
- The feasibility-based sample target has been assessed.
- Targeted supplementary searches have been completed where justified.
- Feasible historical recovery has been attempted.
- Candidate records have undergone eligibility and duplicate review.
- Uncertain records have been documented.
- Final source coverage and data quality have been assessed.

If the sample target cannot be reached, the shortfall and its reasons must be reported.

Unsuitable or unverifiable advertisements must not be included merely to increase dataset size.

The research follows a structured quantitative analytical approach.

The general analytical workflow is:

1. Research design and source governance.
2. Data acquisition and provenance recording.
3. Eligibility and duplicate validation.
4. Data cleaning and standardisation.
5. Job-family classification.
6. Skills, qualification and experience extraction.
7. Descriptive statistical analysis.
8. Comparative and inferential analysis.
9. Multivariable analysis where justified.
10. Temporal and geographic analysis.
11. Interpretation and reporting.
12. Publication of permitted aggregate findings.

`docs/02_Research_Methodology/Statistical_Methodology.md`

### 14.1 Statistical Integrity

All statistical methods must be selected according to:

- Research question.
- Variable type.
- Data completeness.
- Sample adequacy.
- Statistical assumptions.
- Independence and potential overlap.
- Interpretability.
- Practical relevance.

The project will not force an analysis when available evidence is insufficient.

## 15. Research Limitations

The following limitations are recognised in the approved research design.

### 15.1 Advertisement-Based Evidence

Job advertisements represent advertised requirements and opportunities, not actual recruitment or hiring outcomes.

### 15.2 Non-Probability Sampling

The project uses a non-probability sample of accessible eligible advertisements.

The resulting dataset must not be described as a statistically representative sample of all South African technology vacancies.

### 15.3 Source Coverage

Recruitment platforms and employer career pages may differ in coverage, accessibility and historical availability.

### 15.4 Missing Information

Advertisements may omit salary, qualifications, experience, benefits or other relevant details.

Missingness may affect the suitability of individual analyses.

### 15.5 Duplicate Detection

Duplicate detection may not identify every repeated advertisement, particularly where job descriptions or titles have changed.

### 15.6 Classification Limitations

Job-family and skill classifications may involve ambiguity and require human review.

### 15.7 Geographic Ambiguity

Remote opportunities and advertisements covering multiple locations may not provide enough information for precise provincial classification.

### 15.8 Historical Recovery

Historical job advertisements may be inaccessible, incomplete or difficult to verify.

### 15.9 International Remote Opportunities

International remote opportunities are included only when South African work eligibility is explicitly supported by the advertisement.

### 15.10 Temporal Interpretation

The twelve-month research window supports retrospective monthly and quarterly comparisons but does not, by itself, establish long-term market trends or reliable seasonality.

## 16. Ethical, Governance and Publication Considerations

The research will follow the approved data governance principles.

These include:

- Responsible data acquisition.
- Respect for source terms and permitted use.
- Data provenance and traceability.
- Data minimisation.
- Appropriate handling of personal information.
- Restricted access to raw data where redistribution is not permitted.
- Transparent data quality and methodological limitations.
- Reproducibility of analytical procedures.
- Publication of permitted aggregate findings.

The project will not assume that publicly accessible information is automatically unrestricted for redistribution.

Raw advertisements and source material will remain private where source permissions or applicable restrictions require this.

Public-facing research outputs must be reviewed for privacy, source restrictions and small-cell disclosure risks.
